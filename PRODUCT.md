# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Engineers shipping with AI agents — from solo developers moving fast to compliance-sensitive engineering teams and orgs with HIPAA, PCI, or SOC2 obligations. They use Cursor, Claude Code, Copilot, or local models. They know git. They don't need hand-holding — they need a gate that runs every time without getting in the way.

## Product Purpose

ComplianceGate is the governance gap layer between AI-assisted coding and git push. It watches for PHI, PAN, secrets, and policy violations before they enter git history — at three gates (watch, commit, push) with a local-first audit trail. It exists because AI agents commit fast and PR review is too late.

## Brand Personality

Authoritative, precise, on your side. The gate is strict because the stakes are real — not corporate compliance theater, but a sharp tool that runs every time. Think: a firm handshake from someone who has read the contract. Same Gilded Service family as PM Butler, different job: governance, not management.

## Anti-references

Generic SaaS compliance dashboards (Vanta, Drata visual language — navy/blue, enterprise-friendly, soft corners). GitHub-adjacent dev tools that trend flat gray and minimal. Buzzy AI sites with metric-heavy heroes, identical icon-card grids, and eyebrow text above every section.

## Design Principles

1. Gate as guardian — the product is strict because the stakes are real; the design carries that gravity without being forbidding.
2. Show the output, not promises — the demo terminal is the proof; lead with what the tool actually produces.
3. Local-first trust — decisions stay on your machine; the design should feel as solid and self-contained as the product itself.
4. Precision over decoration — tight copy, intentional spacing, no scaffolding that doesn't carry information.
5. Same family, different job — shares the Gilded Service language with PM Butler but speaks governance, not management.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG AA minimum. prefers-reduced-motion respected for all animations. Severity levels labeled in text alongside color — color is never the sole indicator.
